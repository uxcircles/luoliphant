Home Office clarifies the new 10-year long residence rules

Applications made after the April 2024 changes were held while the department worked out what the new rules meant. Its clarified position turns out to be a little more generous than feared.

Three tests instead of two

Absences over the 10 years are now split into two periods, before and after 11 April 2024. You must satisfy all three:

184days maximum for any single absence that started before 11 April 2024
548days maximum in total across the part of the qualifying period before 11 April 2024
180days maximum outside the UK in any rolling 12 months from 11 April 2024

The Home Office's worked examples

Example 1 · Whole qualifying period before 11 April 2024

The applicant completed 10 years on 10 April 2024, with two absences of 50 and 120 days, both finished before 11 April 2024. Neither exceeded 184 days and the total stayed under 548 days, so continuous residence is not broken.

Example 2 · Started before 11 April 2024, completed after

The applicant completed 10 years on 20 January 2025 with five absences. The three completed before 11 April 2024 are tested against the 184-day single and 548-day total limits. An absence from 1 April to 1 July 2024 started before the change, so the 184-day single limit applies, and its days up to 10 April count towards the 548-day total. The absence from 1 August 2024 to 20 January 2025 is tested against 180 days in any rolling 12 months, starting from 1 August 2024. None breaks the rules, so residence is continuous.

Example 3 · Qualifying period started on or after 11 April 2024

The applicant completes 10 years on 30 April 2034 with one absence from 1 July to 10 August 2030. Only the 180-days-in-12-months rule applies, and it is met.

What this means for you

If your application relies on residence before 11 April 2024, a total of more than 548 days, or a single absence of more than 184 days, in that period will break continuous residence. Your options are then to wait until those absences fall outside the 10-year window, or to show exceptional circumstances beyond your control, such as illness or pandemic travel disruption.

The new 12-month rule

If your current visa was granted on or after 11 April 2024, you must also have held it for at least 12 months in the same route. That does not mean waiting a year after every renewal: an extension in the same category counts.

For example, after 7 years on student and graduate visas you switch to a spouse visa for 2.5 years, then extend it for another 2.5. You only need 6 more months to reach 10 years and apply. If instead the relationship ended and you moved to a Skilled Worker visa, you would need 12 months on that visa before applying for long residence.
